# Guitar Techniques

A practice reference: chords, riffs, techniques and tips.

---

## Chords

### Open chords (the foundation)
| Chord | Shape tip |
|---|---|
| **G** | Use fingers 2-1-3 (or 3-2-4 for easier changes to C) |
| **C** | Keep finger 1 arched so the high E rings |
| **D** | Strum only the top 4 strings |
| **A** | Three fingers squeezed on fret 2 |
| **E / Em** | Easiest full-sounding chords; great for rock |
| **Am / Dm** | The "sad" chords; Am → C → G → F is a classic loop |

**Common progressions to practise**
- G – D – Em – C (hundreds of pop songs)
- Am – F – C – G
- E – A – B (12-bar blues in E)

### Barre chords
- **E-shape barre** (root on low E string) and **A-shape barre** (root on A string) let you play any major/minor chord anywhere on the neck.
- Press with the *bony side* of your index finger, not the flat pad.
- Keep your thumb behind the neck, around the middle, not hooked over the top.
- F is the classic hard one: build up strength by holding it for 10 seconds, then releasing.

### Power chords
- Root + fifth (two or three notes). Played on the low E or A string.
- The backbone of rock and punk; sounds great with distortion.
- Mute the unused strings with the underside of your fretting fingers.

### Spicy chords to learn next
- **Add9, sus2, sus4** – open, airy sounds (very common in RHCP / Frusciante parts).
- **Major 7 / minor 7** – jazzy, smooth.
- **Dominant 7 (E7, A7, B7)** – blues and funk.

## Techniques

### Left hand (fretting)
- **Hammer-ons / pull-offs** – sound notes without picking; makes runs smooth and fast.
- **Slides** – move one finger up/down to the next note while keeping pressure.
- **Bends** – push the string up to raise the pitch. Use 2–3 fingers together for support. Check bends are *in tune* against the target note.
- **Vibrato** – small, even wiggles after a note. Gives you a "voice"; practise slow and wide vs fast and narrow.
- **Double-stops** – two notes at once (think *Scar Tissue*).

### Right hand (picking / strumming)
- **Alternate picking** – down-up-down-up, always. Builds speed and consistency.
- **Palm muting** – rest the edge of your picking hand lightly on the strings near the bridge for a chunky, tight sound.
- **Funk "chicken scratch"** – fret a chord lightly, keep strumming 16th notes, and only press down on the accented hits. Core RHCP skill.
- **Fingerpicking** – thumb on bass strings, fingers on treble. Start with simple patterns (Travis picking).
- **Hybrid picking** – pick + middle/ring fingers together.

### Advanced / modern
- **Tapping** – use a picking-hand finger to hammer notes on the fretboard.
- **Thumping and slapping** – percussive, Tim Henson-style techniques; slap the low strings with the thumb, pop higher strings.
- **Harmonics** – touch (don't press) the string right above fret 12, 7 or 5 for bell-like notes.

## Riffs and exercises to practise
*(Learn the actual song riffs from licensed tab sites or lessons. These are original exercises.)*

- **Chromatic warm-up (1-2-3-4)** – one finger per fret, play frets 1-2-3-4 on every string, then back down. Use a metronome, start at 60 bpm.
- **Minor pentatonic box 1** – the most-used scale in rock/blues. In A: start at fret 5. Learn it up and down, then improvise over a backing track.
- **Spider exercise** – fingers 1-3 on one string, 2-4 on the next. Builds independence.
- **Muting drill** – play a 16th-note strum with the left hand resting on the strings (no notes), then add chord hits on beats 2 and 4. Trains funk rhythm.
- **Chord-change minute** – pick two chords (e.g. G → C). Count how many clean changes you make in 60 seconds. Try to beat your score each day.

## Practice tips
1. **Metronome always.** Play slow and clean first; speed comes later.
2. **Short and daily beats long and rare.** 20 focused minutes a day > 3 hours once a week.
3. **Record yourself.** You hear mistakes on a recording that you miss while playing. GarageBand works well for this.
4. **Learn songs you love.** Motivation matters more than the "perfect" curriculum.
5. **Relax your grip.** Squeezing too hard slows you down and hurts your hands.
6. **Check your tuning** before every session, and check intonation if chords sound off higher up the neck.
7. **Split practice into blocks:** warm-up → technique → song → free improv.

## Tone and recording tips (GarageBand)
- Record a **clean DI signal** and add amp sims after (for example, a Neural DSP plugin), so you can change the tone later.
- Set input levels so the loudest notes peak around **-6 dB**. Never let it clip (go red).
- Use **Channel EQ**: cut mud around 200–400 Hz, roll off below ~80 Hz, add a little presence around 2–5 kHz.
- **Double-track** rhythm parts (record twice, pan left and right) for a huge sound.
- Less gain often sounds *bigger* on a recording than tons of distortion.
