# Chess

Openings, piece values and tips.

---

## Piece values
| Piece | Value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Pawn | **1** | Gets stronger as it advances; promotes on the last rank |
| Knight | **3** | Best in closed positions with lots of pawns; only piece that jumps |
| Bishop | **3** (≈3.25) | Best in open positions; the **bishop pair** is worth about an extra half pawn |
| Rook | **5** | Loves open files and the 7th rank |
| Queen | **9** | Most powerful piece, but don't bring it out too early |
| King | ∞ | Can't be traded; becomes an active fighter in the endgame (worth about 4) |

**Common trades to know**
- Bishop or knight for 3 pawns ≈ even.
- Rook for a minor piece + 1–2 pawns ≈ even ("the exchange").
- Two rooks ≈ queen + 1 pawn.

## Openings I like

### As White
| Opening | Moves | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Italian Game** | 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4 | Fast development, aims at the weak f7 square, easy to learn |
| **Ruy Lopez** | 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5 | Classic; puts long-term pressure on Black's centre |
| **Queen's Gambit** | 1.d4 d5 2.c4 | Offers a pawn to take the centre; solid and strategic |
| **London System** | 1.d4, then Bf4, e3, Nf3, c3 | Same setup against almost anything; low memorisation |

### As Black
| Against | Opening | Moves | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.e4 | **Sicilian Defence** | 1…c5 | Sharp and unbalanced; plays for a win |
| 1.e4 | **Caro-Kann** | 1…c6 2.d4 d5 | Very solid, hard to crack |
| 1.e4 | **French Defence** | 1…e6 2.d4 d5 | Solid, counterattacks White's centre |
| 1.d4 | **King's Indian** | 1…Nf6 2.c4 g6 | Lets White take the centre, then attacks it |
| 1.d4 | **Queen's Gambit Declined** | 1…d5 2.c4 e6 | Rock-solid and classical |

### Opening principles (more important than memorising lines)
1. **Control the centre** (e4, d4, e5, d5).
2. **Develop knights and bishops** before moving the same piece twice.
3. **Castle early**, usually within the first 10 moves.
4. **Don't bring the queen out early.** It gets chased around and you lose time.
5. **Connect your rooks** by clearing the back rank.

## Tactics to recognise
- **Fork** – one piece attacks two at once (knights are the kings of forks).
- **Pin** – a piece can't move without exposing a more valuable piece behind it.
- **Skewer** – like a pin in reverse: the valuable piece is in front and has to move.
- **Discovered attack** – moving one piece reveals an attack by another.
- **Double check** – two pieces give check at once; the king *must* move.
- **Back-rank mate** – a king trapped behind its own pawns gets mated by a rook or queen.
- **Removing the defender** – capture or distract the piece that guards something.

## Tips I've learned
1. **Before every move, ask "What does my opponent threaten?"** This alone prevents most blunders.
2. **Check every Check, Capture and Threat** (for both sides) before choosing a move.
3. **Don't grab "free" material without checking** that it isn't a trap.
4. **When ahead in material, trade pieces** (not pawns) to simplify into a won endgame.
5. **When behind, keep pieces on** and create complications.
6. **Knights on the rim are dim.** Knights on the edge control fewer squares.
7. **Rooks belong on open files**, and behind passed pawns.
8. **Have a plan.** Even a simple one ("attack the kingside", "trade into an endgame") beats random moves.
9. **Review your losses.** Run games through an engine and find the *one* moment it went wrong.
10. **Puzzles daily.** 10–15 tactics puzzles a day improves pattern recognition faster than anything else.

## Endgame basics
- **King + Queen vs King** and **King + Rook vs King** – learn these checkmates first.
- **Opposition** – in king-and-pawn endings, the king that *doesn't* have to move often wins.
- **Activate your king** in the endgame; it's a strong piece once the queens are gone.
- **Passed pawns must be pushed.**
- **Rule of the square** – a quick way to tell if a king can catch a running pawn.
